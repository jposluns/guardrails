Evidence and assurance

The proof behind the claims.

AIQT asks your assistant to back a claim with evidence, so this page holds AIQT to the same rule. Here is what is available now, what has been tested, and what is documented. Where a fact is not yet published, the page says so plainly rather than implying it.

The release

AIQT 1.0.5

  • Version: 1.0.5, the pack release. The chat-assistant Skill it ships is versioned on its own: the chat skill now served from the install page is 1.0.6 under the Apache License 2.0.
  • The artefact: the packaged skill zip, plus a portable instruction file, both on the install page.
  • Built from: the pack release is marked by v1.0.5, a retroactive tag pointing at the commit of 2026-09-18 where the version became 1.0.5; the tree at that tag carries the Elastic License 2.0 it shipped with, not the project's current licence. The chat skill is generated by tools/gen_skill.py, and its provenance record names the source corpus hash.
  • Checksum: the Skill zip's SHA-256 digest is listed on the install details page, and release hashes are published independently on posluns.dev. The 1.0.5 pack itself was not released through the RELEASING.md protocol, so it has no recorded pack artefact digests.

Known limitations

What AIQT does not do

AIQT is a behavioural standard your assistant is required to follow, not a control that technically prevents a model from erring. Results still depend on the model, the platform, competing instructions, and the tools available in the conversation. AIQT does not make an AI infallible, and it does not replace your judgement, your organization's policy, or the law.
