You and your chat assistant

Add AIQT to your assistant.

Your assistant will sound just as confident when it is guessing as when it knows. AIQT 1.0.0 fixes that, and it is available now as one skill for chat assistants. The install is the same everywhere: get the skill from the guardrails repository and add it to any assistant that can take one. Claude, ChatGPT, and Copilot are among them. From then on, every conversation is held to the standard.

What you get

What changes once it is on

It shows its work

Every factual claim points back to a source, and when a guardrail catches something, your assistant names the guardrail and what it caught.

It stops and asks

When your assistant does not know, it says so. When a decision is yours, it pauses and asks, instead of filling the gap with a plausible invention.

"Done" is verified

A "done" rests on a check that actually ran, so every finished claim comes with a check you can point to.

Nothing changes silently

No weakened checks, no half-built work dressed up as finished, no invented names or citations. Failures are surfaced first.

For the platforms we test, we publish a conformance report: how that platform performed under the standard, so you can see the results before you rely on it.

Beyond chat

Working in a codebase?

Writing code? The development assistant (1.1.0, in development) brings the same standard into your repository: it generates the file each coding agent reads and writes only inside its own directory. Teams can share one standard across every assistant they use, on the Teams & Enterprise path.
