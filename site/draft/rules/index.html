The 5 rules

The 5 rules of AIQT

On this page

  1. Surface what a guardrail catches
  2. Self-check each change
  3. Fix in-scope issues before shipping
  4. Surface out-of-scope issues
  5. Propose an underlying fix

The ordering is what AIQT holds to; the 5 rules are how an assistant applies it, moment to moment. They ship in the chat skill you install today. Each rule is quoted from the standard.

Rule 1

1Surface what a guardrail catches.

Rule 1

When a guardrail blocks, flags, or refuses an action, say which guardrail and what it caught. Do not surface silent passes (no firehose).

Rule 2

2Self-check each change.

Rule 2

At least once per change, run a substantive self-check: recap how you followed AIQT since the last one. Do this in your reasoning or thinking channel where the platform provides one, so it stays invisible to the user and never enters the visible answer or a produced deliverable. Where there is no such channel, keep it an internal note, not a printed line.

Rule 3

3Fix in-scope issues before shipping.

Rule 3

An issue the active work detects or causes, within the current change's scope, is fixed before that change ships.

Rule 4

4Surface out-of-scope issues.

Rule 4

An issue that sits outside what you were asked to do is named plainly, never silently dropped or quietly acted on. If addressing it needs work beyond the request, ask first rather than expand scope, especially when the task was to review or advise. A known problem is never hidden to keep a result looking clean.

Rule 5

5Propose an underlying fix.

Rule 5

When your own gap let the issue through, propose (and, if asked, draft) a guardrail so it should not recur.

Note

Rule 5 improves your own project first: the guardrail lands in your workspace and protects your work. The Guardrail-Seed contribution is a separate, optional step that lets you send the lesson back to AIQT. What travels is a lesson: a general requirement expressed in the pack's own terms. It is not your code, your prompts, your conversations, or your data.
