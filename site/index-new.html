Governance you own. Depth you compose.

Accuracy, Integrity, Quality & Trust before progress, speed, and cost.

AIQT is a governance pack for AI-assisted work. It is one priority ordering and a set of disciplines that decide how an AI assistant works: what it must verify before it may claim something is done, when it must stop and ask rather than guess, what it may never quietly change, and what has to end up in the record.

(Accuracy = Integrity = Quality = Trust) > Progress > Speed > Cost

One ordering, decided in advance

When correctness and craft pull against being fast or being cheap, correctness wins, every time. Deciding that in advance means nobody has to re-argue it under a deadline. The top tier has four facets, and they are co-equal: there is no ranking among them, because an accurate but fabricated result, or a high-quality but dishonest one, does not exist. If two ever seem to conflict, that is a framing problem to surface, not a trade to make.

Accuracy

Every factual claim matches its source. Every statement about the state of something rests on an observation, not a guess. "Done" means a check actually ran. If a fact is unknown, we say so rather than invent something plausible.

Integrity

The work is what it appears to be. No stubbed or mocked results dressed up as finished, no quietly weakened checks, no fabricated names or citations, no silent changes. When something fails, we surface it.

Quality

The work meets a standard of craft: correct against the requirements, consistent with the conventions, and complete across every surface a change touches, not only the one that was easy to remember.

Trust

Trust is warranted by the record and granted by a person. It is never claimed by the assistant. Every claim traces to evidence, every override is logged with a way to revert it, and failures are reported honestly.

Then Progress. Just below the top tier sits decisive advancement: when the answer is derivable, decide and act, rather than over-deliberating or grinding marginal work. Progress never licenses less verification. It is about not spinning in place, not about cutting checks.

The five rules of AIQT

The working form of the ordering, scoped to the issues the active work detects or causes. Following the ordering is not about draining a backlog; it is about how the work in front of you is done.

  1. You talk about AIQT.

    When a guardrail catches something, say so: which guardrail, and what it caught. Silent passes stay quiet, so the signal does not become noise.

  2. You talk about AIQT.

    Remind yourself, as an assistant, that you always follow AIQT. The same opening as the first rule, turned inward: a short self-check, at least once a change.

  3. Fix issues.

    An issue the active work detected or caused, within the current change's scope, is fixed before that change ships.

  4. Fix other issues.

    An issue the active work detected or caused but outside the current change's scope is fixed in the next one, after the current change is finished.

  5. Fix underlying issues, and share the fix.

    When a guardrail gap let the issue through, create or improve a guardrail so it should not recur, in addition to fixing the instance. Then, with permission, the portable guardrail can be contributed back, so every assistant improves. Sharing is opt-in.

What AIQT is, and is not

What it is

  • A governance pack for AI-assisted work: one priority ordering, a set of disciplines, and one universal skill.
  • A thin, language-neutral security floor: never hardcode a secret, validate at every trust boundary, contain before you open, gate the pipeline in order.
  • Traceable: rules connect to real maintenance events, not to abstractions.

What it is not

  • A per-language secure-coding ruleset that tries to cover every case. A governance discipline and a secure-coding rule fail differently and are maintained on different clocks.
  • "Never weaken a check to silence a failure" needs no update when a framework ships a new version. Per-language depth has a shelf life in months.
  • So that depth stays the specialists' work, and AIQT composes it.

Composed Depth: discovery, not endorsement

Breadth is built by composition, not by AIQT owning every language and framework. When you set up a project, the installer detects your stack and shows the aligned sources that apply, with their coverage, licence, and last-reviewed date, and the gaps each one leaves. You choose: take all that apply, or decide one by one. AIQT lists a source and shows the facts. It does not bless it, and it credits and links out to the people who do that work.

Where AIQT is now, and where it is going

Available now

The chat assistant

AIQT 1.0.0 is one skill you load into a chat assistant that accepts one (Claude, ChatGPT, and Copilot among them), and it holds that assistant to a stated standard: claims match their sources, checks are not quietly weakened, and work is finished rather than declared finished. Every platform we test carries a published conformance report, so that once a platform is tested you can see how it performed before you rely on it.

Next

The development assistant

Version 1.1.0 installs the same governance core into a project instead of pasting it into a chat. It generates the file each coding agent expects, sets itself up through a wizard, and ships a doctor that verifies the install. It writes only inside its own directory, so nothing else in your repository changes without your say-so. It integrates with your CI, and its findings loop validates a finding before acting on it rather than after.

Ideas we are considering

No commitments, no dates

A web console would show governance activity across projects without requiring a terminal. Enterprise management would cover what an organization needs and an individual does not: central policy, shared configuration, and reporting across teams. We move an idea into a release only when we can say what it does and show that it works.

Open, portable, and shared

AIQT is published under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0. The guardrails are portable across toolchains, and improvements come back under the same ShareAlike licence, so a fix one team makes becomes a fix everyone inherits. That is the system working.
