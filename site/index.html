Rules your AI follows. Controls that catch what slips.

AIQT Guardrails holds your assistant to one standard: Accuracy, Integrity, Quality, and Trust. More than guidance: these principles outrank progress, speed, and cost. In chat, your assistant answers to that standard. In your codebase, technical controls block, flag, or refuse the mistakes they are built to catch. Free and open.

What AIQT fixes

Four everyday failures, and what changes

Each one maps to a facet of the standard: what happens unguarded, and what happens once the guardrails are in place.

Accuracy

Unguarded

A gap in the assistant's knowledge gets filled with a confident, plausible guess, and the guess reads exactly like a fact.

Guarded

The rules require claims to be verified at their source or labelled as unverified. The answer tells you what was checked, what was not, and what would settle the difference.

Integrity

Unguarded

A failing check gets quietly skipped, a broken test gets deleted, an inconvenient result gets smoothed over so the work can keep moving.

Guarded

The rules treat masking a failure as worse than reporting one, and in a repository the shipped controls flag or refuse the silent shortcut. Failures surface where you can see them.

Quality

Unguarded

"Done" means the happy path ran once. Edge cases, error handling, and the checks nobody re-ran are left for the next person to discover.

Guarded

Done is a defined bar, not a feeling. The rules hold work to the bar, and in a repository the controls block the claim of completion until the checks that back the claim have actually run.

Trust

Unguarded

The assistant reports more than the evidence supports: "all tests pass" when some never ran, "fully fixed" when one case was patched.

Guarded

An overclaim is treated as a defect. The rules require the report to match the evidence, and the shipped controls are built to catch claims that reach beyond what was verified.

One standard

One ordering, and it does not bend

(Accuracy = Integrity = Quality = Trust) > Progress > Speed > Cost

Accuracy, Integrity, Quality, and Trust sit together as one top tier, and that tier outranks Progress, Speed, and Cost, in that order. The ordering is the whole point: a gain in progress, speed, or cost never buys a loss on the top tier. When the two collide, your assistant is instructed to stop and tell you rather than resolve the conflict silently. That single ordering is what every rule and every control in the pack serves.

How it works

Instruction and enforcement, one standard from both sides

  1. One standard, written down.

    The pack ships the standard as rules your assistant actually works from: what counts as verified, what counts as done, what must never be smoothed over. Not a poster on the wall; the working instructions themselves.

  2. The rules ride along.

    In chat, AIQT loads the rules into the conversation, so every answer is produced under the standard rather than checked against the standard afterwards. In a repository, the adapter puts the same rules where your coding assistant reads before acting.

  3. Controls catch what slips.

    Rules guide; controls enforce. In your codebase, shipped hooks, lints, and gates watch for the specific mistakes they are built to catch, and block, flag, or refuse them at the moment they happen.

Standards crosswalk

Mapped to the weaknesses developers already know

AIQT's security rules cross-map to 72 MITRE CWE weaknesses (CWE 4.20), MITRE's catalogue of software weakness types. Each mapping is tagged as a tight or broad fit, and every mapped identifier is validated against the pinned edition before it ships.

It is a curated subset, not exhaustive CWE coverage, and a mapping states a relationship of ideas, not certification or compliance. CWE is one of 17 frameworks in the crosswalk, alongside NIST, ISO, CSA, OWASP, and MITRE ATLAS. See the full crosswalk.

Better with every release

The catch grows with the pack

AIQT catches the mistakes that align to the rules and process we have built so far, and improves with every release. The pack is open, so you can contribute your own guardrails today. Opt-in tools coming in an upcoming release will let you automatically share the comments, improvements, and guardrails you have added, so the community's contributions feed future versions.

Know what you downloaded

Confirm you have the files we intended

Every release ships with a per-file manifest and a root digest. After downloading, validate the per-file manifest and root digest (also on posluns.dev) to confirm the files are the ones we intended you to have.
