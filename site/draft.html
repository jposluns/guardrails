What changes

What changes when AIQT is on

Think of the last time your assistant let you down. It was almost certainly one of three failures: a confident claim with nothing behind it, unchecked work called done, or a detail that changed between one reply and the next. AIQT holds your assistant to a standard on all three.

Your assistant shows its work

Your assistant is required to tie each factual claim to a source, and when a guardrail catches something, it tells you which guardrail and what it caught. You can see the evidence: the source behind a claim, the check behind a done, the named guardrail behind a catch.

It stops and asks when unsure

When your assistant does not know something, it says so plainly. When a decision needs your judgement, it pauses and asks. A plausible-sounding invention never fills the gap.

A "done" you can rely on

AIQT requires that "done" rests on a check that actually ran. Your assistant verifies before it declares, so every "done" comes with a check you can point to.

Nothing changes silently

No quietly weakened checks, no half-built results dressed up as finished, no invented names or citations. When something fails, you hear about it before anyone else does.

The honest boundary

What AIQT can and cannot do

AIQT is a behavioural standard, delivered as instructions your assistant is required to follow. With it on, the assistant is required to check before claiming, cite before asserting, ask before deciding for you, and announce every change. Results still depend on the model, the platform, competing instructions, and the tools available in the conversation. AIQT does not make an AI infallible, does not technically prevent a model from erring, and does not replace your judgement, your organization's policy, or the law. What it changes is what you can demand and check: a claim with a source, a "done" with a check, a catch with a name.

The standard

The standard, in plain terms

Four things you can expect from an assistant that follows AIQT. Together they are the top tier of the ordering, and they always come first.

  • Accuracy: claims match their sources, and "done" means a check actually ran.
  • Integrity: nothing is faked, weakened, or changed silently.
  • Quality: correct, consistent, and complete across every surface a change touches.
  • Trust: earned by the record and granted by you, one evidenced claim at a time.
The four facets, in full

Accuracy

What it says matches what is true. Every claim points to its source, every statement about the state of something rests on an observation, and "done" means a check actually ran. An unknown is stated as an unknown.

Integrity

The work is what it appears to be. Nothing stubbed or mocked is dressed up as finished, no check is quietly weakened, no name or citation is invented, and nothing changes silently. Failures are surfaced.

Quality

The work meets a standard of craft: correct against the requirements, consistent with the conventions, and complete across every surface a change touches.

Trust

Trust is warranted by the record and granted by you, one evidenced claim at a time. Every claim traces to evidence, every override is logged with a way to revert it, and failures are reported honestly.

(Accuracy = Integrity = Quality = Trust) > Progress > Speed > Cost

How decisions are made. The four facets are the top tier, co-equal, with no ranking among them. Below them, in order, come three more: Progress (decide and act when the answer is already clear), Speed (do it with fewer wasted steps), and Cost (do it cheaply). When two of these pull against each other, the higher one wins, every time. That call is made once, up front, so nobody re-argues it under a deadline, and Progress, Speed, and Cost never buy their way past accuracy or craft: verification is never the thing that gets cut.
