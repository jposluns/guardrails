Record, render, check, commit.

Operational records, governed like code.

Project records kept in chat logs and hand-edited Markdown drift from what the tools did, so nobody can cite them in a review, an audit, or a handover. OPFiles pairs machine-written TOML records with generated human-readable views. A drift gate, configured as a required check, re-renders the declared views and fails the build when a view and its sources disagree.

What it gives you

OPFiles keeps each record in a fixed, machine-written form and generates the readable version from it; a check, set as a required step, fails the moment the 2 disagree. The standard forbids reusing a record's number or deleting a record. It is open source under the Apache License 2.0, except vendored third-party material, which remains under its own terms. The specification is a working draft; 2 of its 10 commands are not yet implemented, and adoption cannot finish yet.
